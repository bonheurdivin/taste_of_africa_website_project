Who we are

A continent of flavours and stories

Africa is home to an extraordinary diversity of cultures, ingredients and culinary traditions. From the aromatic tagines of North Africa to the rich stews of West Africa and the traditional dishes of East Africa, food tells the story of the continent's people.

Yet African cuisine is often represented through only a small selection of familiar dishes. Tastes of Africa was created to celebrate more of this diversity and make African culinary traditions easier to discover.

We believe that understanding food is also a way of understanding history, identity and the communities that preserve these traditions.

Why we exist

Our mission

To celebrate and promote the richness of African culinary heritage by sharing authentic food stories, traditional recipes and cultural knowledge with audiences around the world.

Through accessible information and engaging experiences, we aim to encourage people to explore unfamiliar dishes, appreciate regional differences and recognise the cultural value of African food.

A rich heritage

How African cuisine tells our history

African food traditions have developed over generations, shaped by local agriculture, geography, migration, trade and cultural exchange. Ingredients such as millet, sorghum, cassava, yams, teff and plantains have played important roles in different communities.

Trade routes and contact between communities also introduced new ingredients and cooking influences. These exchanges helped shape the distinctive cuisines found across the continent.

Today, traditional dishes continue to connect families and communities to their heritage, while contemporary African chefs are bringing these flavours to new audiences.

Beyond the continent

African cuisine around the world

African culinary traditions have influenced food cultures far beyond the continent. Migration, trade and historical connections have helped spread African ingredients, cooking methods and flavours across the globe.

From the global popularity of jollof rice to dishes shaped by West African and Central African culinary traditions in the Caribbean and the Americas, African food continues to inspire chefs, restaurants and home cooks.

We celebrate this influence while recognising the communities and histories that make these food traditions meaningful.

Explore a food landmark

Discover Jemaa el-Fnaa, Morocco

Located in Marrakech, Jemaa el-Fnaa is a famous public square known for its food stalls, traditional performers and lively market culture. It offers a glimpse into Morocco's vibrant street-food traditions.
