Ethiopian Injera

A soft, tangy and naturally fermented flatbread made from teff, bringing people together around the table.

A taste of Ethiopia

More than just a flatbread

Injera is a central part of Ethiopian food culture. Made primarily from teff flour, this fermented flatbread is known for its soft, spongy texture, slightly sour flavour and distinctive honeycomb-like holes.

Injera is traditionally served on a large shared platter with dishes such as spicy stews, lentils and vegetables. Pieces of the bread are torn off and used to scoop up the accompanying food, making meals an important experience of sharing and togetherness.

Let's get cooking

Cooking instructions

1. Prepare the batter

Place the teff flour in a large, clean mixing bowl. Gradually add the lukewarm water while stirring until you have a smooth mixture without lumps.

2. Add the starter

Stir in the ersho starter if available. If using the yeast alternative, add the instant yeast instead and mix thoroughly. Cover the bowl loosely with a clean cloth or lid.

3. Allow fermentation

Leave the batter in a suitable clean container at room temperature for approximately 48â€“72 hours. Fermentation time varies with temperature and the starter used. The batter should develop bubbles and a pleasantly tangy aroma. Discard it if you see mold or notice an unpleasant or rotten smell.

4. Prepare the batter for cooking

Stir the fermented mixture. Pour off excess surface liquid if necessary. Add a little water gradually until the batter reaches a thin, pourable consistency. Stir in the salt if desired.

5. Cook the injera

Heat a large non-stick skillet or flat griddle over medium heat. Pour the batter in a circular motion, starting around the outside and moving towards the centre. Spread it into a thin, even layer.

6. Look for the characteristic holes

Cover the pan with a lid. Allow the injera to cook for approximately 2â€“3 minutes. Small holes should appear across the surface as it cooks. The top should look set and the edges should begin to lift. Do not flip it.

7. Serve and enjoy

Carefully remove the injera and place it on a clean surface to cool. Repeat with the remaining batter. Serve with Ethiopian stews, lentils and vegetable dishes.
