Rwandan Isombe

A comforting Rwandan favourite made from cassava leaves, groundnuts and vegetables, slowly cooked into a rich, flavourful dish.

Preparation 20 minutes
Cooking 1 hour 10 minutes
Total time 1 hour 30 minutes
Difficulty Medium

A taste of Rwanda

A beloved dish from the heart of Rwanda

Isombe is a well-known dish in Rwandan cuisine, prepared mainly from cassava leaves that are pounded or finely processed and cooked until tender. It is often enriched with groundnuts, vegetables and sometimes meat or fish.

The dish is enjoyed in different ways across households and communities. Its rich texture and distinctive flavour make it a familiar part of many Rwandan meals, often served with rice, potatoes or other local staples.

What you'll need

Ingredients

Adjust servings
4
  • 500 g commercially prepared, pounded cassava leaves
  • 500 ml chicken or vegetable broth
  • 250 g fresh spinach, washed and chopped
  • 3 medium tomatoes, chopped
  • 1 large onion, chopped
  • 3 garlic cloves, crushed
  • 1 medium green pepper, sliced
  • 1 medium eggplant, cubed
  • 3 tbsp peanut butter
  • 3 tbsp vegetable or palm oil
  • 1 small bunch fresh coriander
  • Salt to taste

Use commercially prepared cassava leaves intended for cooking. If starting with fresh leaves, ensure they have been properly processed and thoroughly cooked according to safe food-preparation guidance.

Let's get cooking

Cooking instructions

1. Prepare the ingredients

Wash and chop the spinach, tomatoes, onion, green pepper and coriander. Prepare the eggplant and crush the garlic. If using frozen prepared cassava leaves, thaw them according to the package instructions.

2. Cook the cassava leaves thoroughly

Place the prepared cassava leaves in a large pot with the broth. Bring to a boil, then maintain a steady simmer until the leaves are thoroughly cooked and tender. Follow the preparation instructions on the cassava-leaf product.

3. Prepare the vegetable mixture

In a separate pan, heat the oil over medium heat. Add the onion and garlic and cook until softened. Add the tomatoes, green pepper and eggplant. Stir and cook until the vegetables begin to soften.

4. Combine the vegetables

Add the vegetable mixture to the pot of cooked cassava leaves. Stir thoroughly to combine the flavours.

5. Add spinach and groundnuts

Add the chopped spinach and continue simmering until tender. Stir the peanut butter with a little warm broth in a separate bowl until smooth, then add it gradually to the pot.

6. Simmer until rich and creamy

Allow the mixture to simmer gently, stirring occasionally, until the peanut butter is fully incorporated and the stew reaches a thick, creamy consistency. Ensure all ingredients are thoroughly cooked.

7. Season and serve

Add salt to taste and sprinkle with fresh coriander. Serve hot with rice, potatoes or another favourite accompaniment.
